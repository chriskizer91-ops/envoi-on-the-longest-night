From the editors

Welcome to the longest night

Three nights before the longest night of the year, the stars over Wickhollow go out. Io, the village witch, keeps the Moonwell and a bundle of letters to the dead that she has never been able to burn. By the end of this guide she will have flown a patched-up skiff over the Ironspire Peaks, folded those letters into a wyrm, and stood at a dead Moonwell against Noctara the Starless.

We played it to the end and opened up the game files to check our work. Every number in these pages is the game’s own: what each spell costs, how hard it hits, how much a Nightrose costs in the far north, and where every last keepsake is lying in the dark.

Read it front to back for a full walkthrough, or jump to what you need. The maps are marked with numbered callouts, and the keepsake list has boxes you can tick as you go. Your ticks stay on this device.

How to read this guide

TIP boxes hold strategy. SECRET boxes point at things the game never tells you. WARNING boxes mean a fight that can go badly if you walk in unready.

Numbers assume the level you’re likely to be at that point. Every move hits about 20% harder per level, so a level ahead of us is a big help.

Spoiler warning: the walkthrough follows the whole story, ending included.

Before you leave the cottageGetting started

Envoi on the Longest Night plays in your browser on a phone, tablet or computer. You walk painted towns and woods, talk to everyone, and fight in a turn-gauge battle system in the spirit of the late PlayStation classics. Here’s everything you need before the first wraith.

1The title screen

  • Continue appears once you have a save. It loads the slot you saved in most recently and puts Io exactly where she stood.
  • New game asks which of the three save slots to use, then plays the prologue and wakes Io in her cottage.
  • Chapters is the game’s level select. Pick The start, Gate 5: the great wraith, Gate 10: Dawnroost, Gate 15: Halcyon or The approach to the finale. “The party comes as the story leaves it there”: the right level, the shards for the next upgrade, and 3 of each herb. You start with no keepsakes, though.
  • Load lists your slots and has Paste a save code, for a game copied from another device.

4Saving and resting

The game saves itself every time you change places, rest, finish a fight, buy herbs or pick something up. You rarely need to think about it.

Rest points refill HP and MP, save, and become the spot you wake at if you lose a fight. They’re all free: Io’s bed, Wickhollow’s Moonwell, Tobb’s Lanternless Inn in Bogmire, the Warden hall at Dawnroost, the shipyard bunkhouse, the sled camp in the frozen pass, Ede’s Lantern House in Misthollow, and the three camps on the world map.

To keep a game somewhere safe, open Menu → Saves → Save code → Copy. The code starts ENVOI1:. On another device, choose Load → Paste a save code.

Warning
Pasting overwrites without asking

A pasted save code goes straight into the slot you’re using. Switch to an empty slot first if you want to keep the game that’s there.

5Losing isn’t the end

There’s no Game Over screen. If the whole party falls, you wake at the last place you rested with full HP and MP, and the game gives back every herb you used in the fight. You only lose the walk back, and the fight’s experience and shards.

So try a hard fight early just to see it. It costs you very little.

6Settings worth knowing

  • Random fights: Fewer, Normal or More. Fewer stretches the walk between fights to 1⅔×; More shortens it to ⅔×. Set it to More when you’re grinding.
  • Words: Slow, Normal, Fast or All at once.
  • Text size: Normal or Large.
  • Map light: Dim, Normal or Bright. Bright helps on a phone outdoors.
  • Music, Effects, Surroundings: Off, Soft, Normal or Loud.
  • Battle frame rate: 30 (the default), 45, 60 or Screen.
  • Watch again: replays the two full cutscenes once you’ve seen them.

Battle school · part 1

Gauges, commands and the windowsHow battles work

Fights happen on a painted stage with the 3D party on one side and the foes on the other. If you’ve played a late-’90s Final Fantasy you’ll feel at home in seconds. There’s one big difference: you can never be rushed.

The Turn gauge

Every fighter has a Turn gauge that fills in real time. Io’s takes 2.4 seconds to fill and Sol’s 2.2. Foes vary from 1.7 seconds (the Towering Bramble) to 3.4 (the great wraith). When a gauge is full, that fighter acts.

The key rule is in the very first fight’s tip: “While anyone acts or chooses, every gauge waits.” Menus pause the battle, so take your time. And when a hero and a foe are ready at the same moment, the hero always goes first.

The commands

Io: Attack, Witchcraft ▸, Moonlore ▸, Summon ▸, Item ▸, Defend, and Flee in wild fights. In Trance, Moonlight appears at the top.

Sol: Attack, Sword Arts ▸, Item ▸, Guard, and Flee. In Trance, Attack becomes Daybreak and High Noon appears. Greyed-out moves tell you why: MP, Heat, once a battle, Sol needs 70 Heat.

Reading enemy HP

The enemy window only shows a bar. For a real number, open any attack and look at the target list: foes are listed with their HP as a percentage. The cursor always starts on the weakest one.

Tip

A foe’s big once-only move fires the first time it starts a turn below a set HP line: the wraiths’ Eclipse at 35%, Halcyon’s Black Noon at 30%. Use the target list’s % to plan a burst that carries it from above the line to zero in one go.

How damage works

Every move has a base power. It’s multiplied by 1.2 for every level above 1, then rolled up or down by as much as 25%. There are no critical hits and no misses. The extra-large damage numbers you’ll see are just big hits, drawn bigger.

Three elements matter: fire (Flame Bolt), moon (Crescent Blades, Moonlight, Lunara) and sun (Flare Cut, Kestrel Stoop, High Noon, Envoi). A weakness multiplies damage by up to 1.5.

Defend, Guard and running

Defend and Guard halve the damage that hero takes until her next turn. Sol’s Guard also pulls single-target hits off Io onto herself.

Flee only appears in random wild fights. Against a pack it works half the time (two in three with the Bogstriders keepsake). Against any Bramble Horror or the Bramble Colossus it always works, because they’re rooted to the spot. A fled fight gives nothing, and anyone knocked out stays down.

Warning
Guard has a hidden cost

Guard shows no price tag, but it takes up to 40 of Sol’s Heat. Don’t Guard when you’re saving up for Envoi or Solar Crest.

Herbs in battle

Either hero can use an herb on her turn, but each kind only once per fight, however many you carry. That’s one Moonpetal, one Lavender, one Mugwort, one Lily and one Nightrose per battle. Herbs don’t use up a Trance turn.

Battle school · part 2

The two systems that win fightsHeat & Trance

Sol’s Heat

0 ······ 70: Sunburn ······ 100

  • Sol starts every fight at 0 Heat (10 or 20 with the right keepsakes).
  • +20 for each Attack, +10 for every hit she takes, +40 from Io’s Moonsteel, +70 from Harvest Moon.
  • Sword Arts spend it: Flare Cut −25, Sunder −20, Ember Rush −40, Kestrel Stoop −40. Solar Crest needs 50 and spends all of it.

Sunburn

At 70 Heat or more, Sol’s Heat number glows and every action she takes hits 1.35× harder. The catch: if she ends an action still at 70+, the heat burns her for 6% of her max HP.

SecretPro move

The game checks Sunburn before it takes the Heat cost. At 75 Heat, Ember Rush gets the full 1.35× bonus and drops Sol to 35, so she never burns. Bank Heat to 70+, then spend it in one big art.

Trance

The pink gauge under each hero fills two ways:

  • +2% for every hit she lands. Multi-hit moves fill it faster: Crescent Blades is five hits, so +10% a cast. Lunara’s and Envoi’s hits count for Io.
  • Taking damage. Lose about three-quarters of her max HP in total and it fills from empty.

Dealing damage alone stops at 99%. The last push always comes from taking a hit. When the gauge fills, Trance starts at the beginning of that hero’s next turn and lasts 4 of her actions.

Warnings: the charged moves

The nastiest enemy moves take two turns. First you see “…gathers Void Sphere. It falls on its next turn.” Then it lands. You always get one turn to answer:

  • Defend / Guard: halves it for that hero.
  • Lunara: 60% heal now, and only 60% damage until she strikes.
  • Envoi: the Folding Ward cancels the whole move.
  • Kestrel Stoop: Sol rises out of reach and can’t be hit at all.

After the fight

The end card shows battle time, damage dealt, experience and sunstone shards, and a level-up box when you earn one: “Every move hits and heals about 20% harder.”

HP and MP carry over to the next fight; there’s no free heal. Anyone knocked out gets up with 10% HP. Rest, cast Moonlore from the menu, or use herbs between fights.

Why levels matter so much

Heroes and foes both grow 1.2× per level, so a fight against foes at your level feels the same at level 3 or level 18. What changes everything is the gap: each level you are ahead makes your hits about 20% bigger against their HP, and their hits a smaller bite out of yours. Spell MP costs never go up, so Io gets more casts as she levels: about 10 Flame Bolts at level 1 and 30 at level 20.

Walking the world map

Once Sol joins, the cottage’s south, west and east paths lead out onto the world map. Io walks it at a steady pace with no running, and you enter a place by walking onto its glowing dot. Random fights come after roughly 4 to 11 seconds of walking, but never within a short radius of a town, camp or the moored Magpie, so you can always reach safety.

Tip

The land is all connected. Once a band is open you can walk anywhere in it: straight to Bogmire without going through the Thornwood, or straight to Misthollow without the frozen pass. Flying is faster, though, and the Magpie never runs into fights.

Top secret

The things the game never tells youSecrets & letters

1 · The red roof

The Crescent Locket’s card says it plainly: “Hidden on top of the red roof in Wickhollow, at the end of Chris’s secret way.” The roof belongs to the red-tiled cottage just south of Nettie’s stall, west of the Moonwell.

  1. Stand on the lane on the cottage’s north side, just east of its tall chimney.
  2. Walk south down the chimney’s east side, onto the roof.
  3. Double back north-west up the slope to the ridge.
  4. Follow the ridge south to its very end, above the front gable.

The path is only a few steps wide, and tap-to-walk may refuse to route there. Steer with the arrow pad or the keys. No story lock: you can take it before the first fight.

Io

“Whoever hid this didn’t want it found from the ground.”

2 · The Thornwood’s hidden trail

The painting only shows the road and the moon-stone clearing, but there are more trails through the thorns than it lets on. Just south of the road, about two-thirds of the way east, a faint trail drops into the dark woods. It curves south-east, then runs east along the bottom of the map to a dead end. That’s where the Warden’s Brooch lies, “sunk in the moss among the thorns.”

The trail from the north-east corner of the moon-stone clearing hides the Frog-Ring the same way.

Paths that look like scenery

Three more keepsakes sit on walkways that look painted on: Dawnroost’s wall-walk (Kettle-Helm), the shipyard’s cove dock (Dockhand’s Gloves) and Misthollow’s balcony bridge (Misthollow Cowl). If it looks like you could walk there, try it.

3 · The five letter wells

4 · Hidden rules

  • Heroes win ties. If a hero and a foe are ready at the same moment, the hero goes first.
  • No crits, no misses. The only things that can dodge are a Guttering wisp, a hovering Sol and Envoi’s ward.
  • Herbs don’t burn Trance. Using an item during Trance doesn’t count toward her four actions.
  • Dealing damage only fills Trance to 99%. The last 1% always comes from taking a hit.
  • A hovering Sol can’t be touched. Even hit-everyone moves skip her while Kestrel Stoop has her in the air.
  • Envoi is always ready in Dawnbreaker. Sol’s Trance pins her Heat at 100, and Envoi doesn’t drain it.
  • Guard costs up to 40 Heat, with no cost shown.
  • Fleeing leaves the fallen down. Only a win gets knocked-out heroes back on their feet, at 10% HP.

5 · Curiosities

  • Places you can’t reach. The world painting shows a walled castle on an island in the inner sea, and badlands with roads and villages to the south-east. Both are wrapped in cold mist for good, even after the last upgrade.
  • Who is Inkblot? Quill tells Io to fix the lamps “before Inkblot starts fretting.” Nobody ever explains.
  • A spell that isn’t there. The game files hold the full visual effect for an Io spell called Moth Veil, but it has no menu slot in this version.
  • The Moonwell pose. Resting at Wickhollow’s Moonwell is the only rest where Io raises her hands in a casting pose.
  • Shortcuts. Once a band is open you can walk the world map straight to Bogmire or Misthollow, skipping the Thornwood and the frozen pass.
Codes corner

There are no cheat codes. The closest thing is Chapters on the title screen, a built-in level select that starts you at any gate with the right level and shards. It doesn’t give you keepsakes, though.

  4. Shop at homeBand-1 shops keep band-1 prices all game. A Nightrose is 173 shards from Nettie and 2,662 from Sorrel.
  5. Losing is freeYou wake at your last rest with full HP, full MP and every herb back. Try a boss early just to see it.
  6. Tune the fight rateSettings → Random fights. More for grinding, Fewer when you just want to explore.
  7. Lunara before, not afterCall her the turn before a big hit lands. The heal is nice; the 60% damage until she strikes is what saves you.
