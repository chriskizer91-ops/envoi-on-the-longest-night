What changes. The painting keeps its place in the world, so the path and Io stay where they are. A smaller version has fewer pixels to stretch over it, so its pixels show bigger. The game draws them sharp, as you chose, so a small version looks blockier, not blurrier. A light version is squeezed harder at the same size (quality 30 instead of 45), so fine detail (grass, pebbles, petals) smooths out; extra light (20) smooths it more.

Detail kept is measured against your painting as you sent it (SSIM, in four close-ups: the path, the oak, the stone wall and the flowers, each version scaled back up smoothly). 12 scenes is twelve wilderness scenes like this one at that setting. Inside the single game file each takes about a third more. Memory is the painting on the phone once it's opened. The original is the WebP you sent; the others are AVIF, like the town maps.

What the numbers say. Weight for weight, keeping every pixel and squeezing harder keeps more of this painting than making it smaller. 100% light weighs about what 75% does (198 KB against 193 KB) and keeps 93% of the detail against 77%. My pick: 100% extra light. On a phone it looks almost like the original at under a third of the weight.
