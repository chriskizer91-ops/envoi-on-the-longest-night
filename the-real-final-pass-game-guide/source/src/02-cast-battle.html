Character profiles

Meet the cast

Only two heroes ever fight, and they fight in very different ways. Io spends MP; Sol builds Heat and spends it. Learn both rhythms and you’ve learned the game.

P. 9

Io, the Witch

Healer · caster · summoner

Wickhollow’s village witch and the heart of the party. Witchcraft deals damage, Moonlore heals and feeds Heat to Sol, and only she can summon. Her Trance is the Lunar Trance.

HP 1,400 → 44,727 · MP 120 → 363 · gauge 2.4 s (levels 1 → 20)

Sol, the last Warden

Sword · tank · Heat

Runs into Wickhollow chasing a wraith and joins after your first fight. No MP: her Sword Arts run on Heat. Her passive, Warden’s Oath, has her take single hits meant for Io at 70% whenever Io is under 25% HP. Her Trance is Dawnbreaker.

HP 1,800 → 57,506 · Heat 0–100 · gauge 2.2 s

Lunara, the Pale Mother

Summon · from the very first fight

She sleeps in Wickhollow’s Moonwell. Once a battle she heals everyone 60%, lifts the fallen at 60%, and cuts all damage to the party to 60% until she strikes on Io’s next turn.

Envoi, the Letter Wyrm

Summon · after Dawnroost

Folded from the letters Io could never burn and lit from Dawnroost’s living node. Needs Sol at 70+ Heat. Its Folding Ward swallows the next enemy move whole, then it strikes for the biggest number in the game.

Halcyon, the Gloam Knight

Foe · Sol’s old teacher

A knight in dark armor with a black blade and a black sun on its hilt. She came north eight years ago and never came back. Sol knows the way she fights.

Noctara the Starless

Final boss

She put out the stars. On the longest night she means to eclipse the moon from Misthollow’s Moonwell, and then the night won’t end.

Battle school

Fights happen in a 3D arena with the party on one side and the foes on the other. If you’ve played a late-’90s Final Fantasy you’ll feel at home in seconds. The big difference: you can never be rushed.

P. 11

The turn gauge

Every fighter has a gauge that fills in real time. When it’s full, that fighter acts. Io fills in 2.4 s, Sol in 2.2 s. Foes range from 1.7 s (the Towering Reach bramble) to 3.4 s (the great wraith).

The rule that matters is in the first fight’s tip: “While anyone acts or chooses, every gauge waits.” Menus pause the battle, so take your time. If a hero and a foe are ready at the same moment, the hero goes first.

The commands

Io: Attack, Witchcraft ▸, Moonlore ▸, Summon ▸, Item ▸, Defend, and Flee in wild fights. In Trance, Moonlight appears at the top.

Sol: Attack, Sword Arts ▸, Item ▸, Guard −40 Heat, and Flee. In Trance, Attack becomes Daybreak and High Noon appears at the top. Against Halcyon at the crossroads a Kestrel command shows up too.

A greyed move tells you why: MP, Sol is down, once a battle, Sol needs 70 Heat, once a fight, already used.

Reading enemy HP

The enemy window shows only a bar. For a number, open any attack and look at the target list: foes are listed with their HP as a percentage, and the cursor starts on the weakest one.

Tip

A foe’s once-only desperation move fires the first time it starts a turn below a set HP line: the wraiths’ Eclipse at 35%, Halcyon’s Black Noon at 30%, the Colossus’s Wrath at 50%. Use the target list to plan a burst that carries it from above the line to zero (or, for Halcyon, to 20%) before it acts again.

How damage works

Every move has a base power. It’s multiplied by 1.2 for every level above 1, then rolled up or down by as much as 25%. There are no critical hits and no misses. The extra-big numbers you see are just big moves drawn bigger.

Three elements matter. Fire: Flame Bolt. Moon: Crescent Blades, Moonlight, Lunara, and any plain Sol strike after Moonsteel. Sun: Flare Cut, Kestrel Stoop’s dive, High Noon, Envoi. A weakness multiplies damage by up to 1.5.

On top of that, these all multiply together: Sunburn ×1.35 (Sol at 70+ Heat), Sundered ×1.25, the Ember-star Lily ×1.1, keepsake damage bonuses, and the Colossus’s bare heart ×2.

Why levels matter so much

Heroes and foes both grow 1.2× a level, so a fight at your level feels the same at 3 or 18. What changes everything is the gap: each level ahead makes your hits about 20% bigger against their HP. Spell costs never go up, so Io gets more casts as she levels: about 10 Flame Bolts per bar at level 1, 30 at level 20.

Defend, Guard, Flee, herbs

Defend (Io) and Guard (Sol) halve the damage that hero takes until her next turn. Guard also pulls every single-target hit aimed at Io onto Sol, each Soul Bolt and Crown Shard included. Guard costs up to 40 Heat (free in Trance).

Flee is only in wild fights. Against a pack it works half the time (two in three with the Bogstriders). Against any Bramble Horror or the Colossus it always works, because they’re rooted. A fled fight gives nothing, and anyone knocked out stays down.

Herbs: either hero can use one on her turn, but each kind only once per fight, however many you carry. Herbs don’t use up a Trance turn.

After the fight

The end card shows battle time, damage dealt, the frame rate, experience and shards, and a level-up box when you earn one. HP and MP carry over; there’s no free heal. Anyone knocked out gets up with 10% HP after a win.

Sol’s Heat & Sunburn

  • Sol starts every fight at 0 Heat (10 or 20 with the Node Sunstone and Ede’s Hearth-Coal).
  • Gains: +20 for each Attack, +10 for every hit she takes, +40 from Io’s Moonsteel, +70 from Harvest Moon.
  • Costs: Flare Cut 25, Sunder 20, Ember Rush 40, Kestrel Stoop 40, Guard up to 40. Solar Crest needs 50 and spends it all.
  • Sunburn: at 70+ Heat every blow she lands hits 1.35×. If she ends any action still at 70+, the heat burns her for 6% of her max HP.
Secret · Pro move

The game checks Sunburn before it takes the Heat cost. At 75 Heat, Ember Rush gets the full 1.35× and drops Sol to 35, so she never burns. Bank Heat to 70+, then spend it in one big art. It works for Flare Cut at 70–94 and Kestrel Stoop’s rise too.

Trance

The pink gauge under each hero fills two ways: +2% for every hit she lands (Crescent Blades is five hits, so +10% a cast; Lunara’s and Envoi’s hits count for Io), and taking damage: about three-quarters of her max HP in total fills it from empty. Dealing damage stops at 99%; the last push always comes from a hit taken. Trance starts at the beginning of her next turn and lasts 4 of her actions.

Secret · Big damage

In Dawnbreaker, Solar Crest is free and always fires at the full 100 Heat: 1,620 base after the bonus, more than Daybreak, and it doesn’t end Trance. The best pattern: Solar Crest, Solar Crest, Solar Crest, then High Noon.

Caution · Charged moves

The nastiest moves take two turns. First: “…gathers Void Sphere. It falls on its next turn.” Then it lands. You always get one turn to answer:

  • Defend / Guard: halves it for that hero.
  • Lunara: 60% heal now, ×0.6 damage until she strikes.
  • Envoi: the Folding Ward cancels the whole move.
  • Kestrel Stoop: Sol rises out of reach and can’t be hit at all.

The charged moves: a bramble’s Lure → Grab, the Colossus’s Siren Bloom → Devour, Halcyon’s Black Noon, Noctara’s Void Sphere.

Tip · Summon timing

Lunara’s shield and Envoi’s ward last only until Io’s next turn. Call them right after a foe starts gathering, not earlier. Any foe’s move that comes first will eat Envoi’s ward. Envoi’s strike doesn’t use up Io’s next turn; Lunara’s does.
