# Moonlight in the Aether — Lore & Party Bible

Sep 30, 2026 · @Chris

## The world

Moonlight in the Aether is a story about letting go: the living Moon carries every soul home, and one grieving witch has decided she will not let hers leave. Every name already in your builds is kept below; what's new fills the gaps between them.

### Auros, the Aether and sunstone

Auros, the Moon, is alive and asleep. On quiet nights its heartbeat hums over the fen. Its light falls so thick that it pools in the upper sky as the Aether, a glowing sea that ships can sail. Where moonlight touches the ground and gathers, it collects in Moonwells.

Sunstone is the opposite: old sunlight buried in the earth, running in veins called the Ember Line. The ancients cut the Warm Roads along those veins and set nodes that still wake when touched. A skiff needs both. Sunstone gives it lift; the Aether gives its crystal sails something to catch. The Magpie rises on sun and sails on moon, and so does the party: a sunsteel sword and a moon-witch.

### Souls and moths

When someone dies, their light rises. Lunara, the Pale Mother, a moth as tall as the chapel, gathers it and carries it up the moonlight to Auros. In this world a moth is a soul on its way home, which is why moths batter at lamps: they mistake every lamp for the Moon.

A soul that dies in deep dark, like the Thornwood, has no light to follow. It wanders, and the dark hollows it out until only hunger is left. That is a Shadow Wraith. It still remembers the Moonwell as home, so it drifts toward the square, and it eats every light on the way.

### The central mystery

A hundred years ago the water rose over Misthollow. Its Moonwell-keeper, a witch, lost her children in the flood. Lunara came for their souls, and the witch would not let them go. She sank her house in the black willows and lit a light in every window to hold them. She is the Drowned Mother, and every lit window in Mother's Hollow is a soul kept back from the Moon.

Windows need fire, so lights go missing. Wickhollow's witchfire ends up in Bogmire's lamps. The Ember Line dims. Fewer souls reach Auros, so more go hollow, and the wraiths multiply. The Mother's last goal is an eclipse: put Auros out so the Pale Mother can never take anyone again. The black sun in the Wraith's Eclipse attack is her sign.

The Witch keeps Wickhollow's Moonwell, the same post the Mother once held at Misthollow. The Mother sees in her exactly who she used to be.

## The party at a glance

The party is a sun-and-moon pair: Sol deals the damage by burning her own health, and the Witch's healing is what turns that health into damage. Lunara is the once-a-battle rescue when a boss hits harder than the heals can keep up with.

| The Witch | Healer, summoner | Garnet and Eiko | MP, Trance | Seven healing spells, light damage magic, both summons |
| Solenne "Sol" Kestrel | Sword DPS | Steiner and Beatrix | Heat, Trance | The party's damage; guards the Witch with her body |
| Lunara, the Pale Mother | Summon | Eidolons | Once per battle | Heals and shields the party, then a huge hit |
| Envoi, the Letter Wyrm | Joint summon | Eidolons | Once, final fight only; costs Sol's Heat | Blocks one attack, then the biggest hit in the game |

The loop in a fight: Sol attacks to build Heat; above 70 Heat she hits 35% harder but burns her own HP every action. The Witch keeps her topped up so she can stay there. Hits taken fill both Trance gauges. When a boss's big attack is coming, the Witch shields first, or calls Lunara.

Nettie and Inkblot fit later as a third slot: Nettie as a status-and-debuff witch, Inkblot as a fast thief.

## The Witch

The Witch keeps Wickhollow's Moonwell, and her rebuilt kit is mostly healing: eight Moonlore spells that grow from a small mend at level 1 to a full restore and a barrier by level 20. Her damage spells stay exactly as they are in the Night square build.

&#91;image: The Witch, pixel sprite sheet: 13 poses\]

### Who she is

She is the youngest keeper the Moonwell has had: bookish, kind, short-sighted, and far braver than she looks. She brews from her garden, flies the Magpie with Inkblot on the rail, and carries a dagger mostly for cutting herbs. Lunara answers her because the keeper's bond is a vow, not a spell, which is why Lunara is the only summon she can call alone. Envoi, in the final fight, needs Sol too.

Her arc mirrors the Drowned Mother's. Both are keepers; both will lose someone. The Mother held on. The Witch's last choice in the story is whether she can let go.

Open question: her name. Your builds call her the Witch, and so does this doc.

### Witchcraft (damage)

Unchanged from the Night square build: Flame Bolt (12 MP, 330), Crescent Blades (24 MP, 5 hits of 88) and Nightbloom Briars (18 MP, 260 and Bound). They scale with level like everything else.

### Lunar Trance: Twin Moons

Trance still fills from damage taken. While it lasts, every Moonlore spell costs half MP and every heal lands on both allies. Moonlight cashes the Trance in for its three moon-strikes, as now. The choice each Trance is the fun part: hold it for doubled healing through a boss's big turn, or spend it for damage.

## Solenne "Sol" Kestrel, the last Ember Warden

Sol is a sunsteel swordswoman whose damage climbs the more of her own health she burns, which is exactly what makes the Witch's healing matter. She is the sun to the Witch's moon.

### Backstory

The Ember Wardens were sworn to the Ember Line: they walked the Warm Roads, kept the nodes lit and escorted sunstone to the shipyards. Each Warden takes a bird's name at the oath, because birds fly on sunlight. Their swords are sunsteel, quenched in a living node so the blade keeps the warmth. In a long fight it heats until it glows, and burns the hand that holds it.

Sol was a foundling at a Warm Roads waystation, raised by the Wardens. Dame Halcyon, the best blade in the order, took her as squire and named her Kestrel: "because you hover before you strike." Eight years ago Halcyon went north into the Gloomfen to find where the Ember Line's warmth was going. She never came back. Nodes have gone dark one by one since, and the order has dwindled to one.

Sol followed the trail of dimmed nodes and missing light to Wickhollow. She has been tracking the Shadow Wraith out of the Thornwood, and reaches the Night square just as the Witch beats it. She's bleeding from her own blade and says the Witch got lucky. The Witch heals her without asking. "I didn't ask." "You didn't have to." Sol stays because the wraiths come from wherever the light is going, and the Witch has a ship.

### Personality

- Blunt and proud. She never asks to be healed, and her battle lines say so ("I'm fine." at 12% HP).
- Distrusts magic: "Magic is just weather with opinions." Softens on this slowly, mostly because of Lunara.
- Dry humor, quietly kind. Feeds Inkblot when she thinks no one is looking.
- Her fear is becoming Halcyon: burning so hot for a cause that nothing of her is left.

### Look

About 24, tall and broad-shouldered, sun-browned freckled skin, short copper-red hair with one long braid wrapped in gold cord, amber eyes, and a burn scar curling up her right forearm from the grip. Brushed bronze half-plate with one large left pauldron crested with kestrel feathers, a burnt-orange tabard with the Warden sun-crest, cream linen sleeves, and a short midnight-blue cape lined in gold that pairs with the Witch's palette. Her sword is a single-edged sunsteel longsword with a sunstone in the crossguard; the edge glows amber, white-hot in Sunburn.

Palette: bronze, burnt orange, cream, midnight blue and amber glow. Warm against the Witch's magenta and Lunara's lavender.

### Heat and Sunburn

- Heat runs 0 to 100. She gains 20 per Attack, 10 each time she's hit, and 40 from the Witch's Moonsteel.
- At 70 Heat or more she is in Sunburn: every hit deals 35% more, and after each of her actions she loses 6% of her max HP.
- Sword Arts spend Heat, which can drop her out of Sunburn. Riding high Heat or cashing it in is her whole game.

## Lunara, the Pale Mother

Lunara is the first moth, Auros's daughter, who carries every soul up the moonlight to the Moon; she is the Witch's own summon and now heals the party when she arrives, before she strikes.

&#91;image: Lunara, the Pale Mother: 12-pose concept sheet\]

### Lore

There is one Pale Mother and many Moonwells; each well is a door she can come through. Wickhollow's is the one she sleeps in. She answers the Witch once per battle for an in-world reason: every minute she spends fighting is a minute she isn't carrying souls home, so she will not stay for more than one blow.

She and the Drowned Mother are the two halves of the theme. The Pale Mother lets go. The Drowned Mother holds on.

### In battle

1. She rises from the nearest Moonwell, as in the Night square build.
2. New, Pale Mother's Embrace: her wings close over the party. Both allies heal 60% of max HP, a fallen ally revives at 60%, and the party takes 40% less damage until her strike. (The damage cut already exists in your code as the 0.6 multiplier.)
3. On the Witch's next turn, Silver Requiem: six moonbeams of 150, then Moonfall for 1,150, scaled with level.
4. She sinks back into the well.

The Embrace is what makes the summon a heal as well as a nuke. Against the tier 3 boss, calling her during Black Noon's wind-up is the cleanest answer to it.

## Envoi, the Letter Wyrm

Envoi is a dragon folded from letters to the dead and lit from inside by Sol's sunsteel. It is the Witch's second summon: made once, in the final fight, and gone when its one blow lands.

&#91;image: Envoi, the Letter Wyrm: model sheet and action sheet\]

### Lore

Every village on the fen leaves letters at its Moonwell for the people Lunara carried away. By custom the keeper burns them on the longest night, so the smoke follows the souls up the moonlight. The Witch never could. Every letter left at Wickhollow's well since she became keeper is still boxed under her bunk on the Magpie.

On the way down into Mother's Hollow she understands why the Drowned Mother frightens her: they are keeping the same thing. She folds the letters into a dragon, and Sol lights its heart with her blade. They call it Envoi, after the last lines of a poem, the part spoken to whoever it is going to.

Envoi answers no vow, so the keeper's one-summon bond doesn't cover it. It needs both of them: the Witch's paper and Sol's fire. It can only be made once, because a letter can only be sent once.

It completes the theme. Lunara carries souls home. The Drowned Mother keeps lights in her windows to hold them back. Envoi is a light that burns itself up to let them go, and it is the Witch learning to do that herself.

### Look

| Part | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Body | 16 folded paper lantern segments in a long S-curve, joined tip to tip with red cord; no legs or arms |
| Head | Large wedge head with long swept horns, a folded frill behind the skull, brow ridges, whisker barbels and glowing gold eyes |
| Seal | A red wax seal on the brow, marked with a crescent cradling a flame; it breaks during the summon |
| Wings | Two pleated paper fans on tan ribs, with scorched, ragged tips |
| Heart | An eight-sided paper lantern hanging under the chest on a red cord; the brightest light on it |
| Tail | Scorched paper streamers and small paper tags tied on red string, shedding embers |
| Surface | Ivory paper written over in faded ink columns, lit gold from inside, with red tassels at every joint |

Palette: ivory paper, black ink, vermilion seal-red and warm lantern gold. No other party member or summon uses this lane.

Size: about 6.2 m to the wing tops and 8 m from snout to streamer tips. It floats about half a meter off the ground.

Locked choices: no legs; the segments stay rigid paper lanterns, not scales; the glow comes from inside the paper, not from a fire aura; the wings stay folding fans, not bat wings.

### In battle

Envoi appears only in Mother's Hollow, once per battle.

1. **Summon, on the Witch's turn.** Sol must be standing with 70 or more Heat; lighting the heart spends all of it. Letters fold into the wyrm from head to tail, Sol's blade lights the heart lantern, and the seal breaks with a roar. Envoi coils in front of the party as a paper wall.
2. **Folding Ward, while it waits.** The wall takes the next enemy attack completely, every target included. The ward ends when it absorbs a hit or when Envoi strikes.
3. **Envoi, when the Witch's gauge next fills.** It wraps around the foe and burns from the tail up as a ring of fire: eight hits of 180, then the heart lantern drops into the center and bursts for 1,800. That is 3,240 at level 1 and about 11,860 at level 20, scaled with the level curve. The Witch then takes her turn as normal.
4. **Against the Drowned Mother,** the rising letters carry off the souls in her windows. She loses every stolen light she was holding, so she can't heal herself until she steals more.
5. **If the battle ends first,** Envoi burns away quietly without striking.

Lunara and Envoi are built to feel different. Lunara heals and shields the whole party and spends the Witch's next turn on her strike. Envoi costs Sol's Heat instead, blocks one attack outright, hits much harder, and leaves the Witch's turn free.

### For the battle code

- Show Envoi in the Summon menu only in Mother's Hollow, greyed out unless Sol is standing with 70 or more Heat.
- On cast: set `B.s.heat` to 0, call `envoi.play('summon')`, and set `B.envoiWard` and `B.envoiPending` to true.
- When an enemy attack resolves while `B.envoiWard` is true: cancel the whole attack, call `envoi.play('block')`, and set `B.envoiWard` to false.
- When the Witch's gauge fills and `B.envoiPending` is true: set `envoi.state.reach` to the enemy's distance, call `envoi.play('envoi')`, apply 180 × the level multiplier at each of the first eight hit times and 1,800 × the multiplier at 0.85, clear the Drowned Mother's stolen lights, then give the Witch her turn.

He pays Bogmire in violet flames so the town looks the other way, and Mayor Gretch takes the deal.

He carries a long brass candle-snuffer pole, and three small caged lanterns hang from his belt, each holding a stolen light. Palette: tallow cream, tarnished brass, soot black, violet flame.

### Level curve

Damage and healing at level L = the level 1 value × (1 + 0.14 × (L − 1)). HP and MP = the level 1 value × (1 + 0.1 × (L − 1)).
