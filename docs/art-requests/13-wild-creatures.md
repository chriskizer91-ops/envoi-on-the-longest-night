# Art Request 13: The Wild's Own Creatures

October 4, 2026, for the final draft's polish (`../../envoi-final-draft/`). Number 12 is the wilderness walking scenes (`12-wilderness-scenes.md`): Chris answered `../questions/open.md` 20 and 21 on October 5, and his eight pictures came the same night.

**Why:** today the wilds of bands 1 and 2 hold only wisps and wraiths, and across the whole game about nine wild fights in ten are against wisps, frost wisps and wraiths, all of them Noctara's. Band 3 adds the Bramble Horror (one fight in five) and band 4 the Bramble Colossus (one in twelve). Chris's direction (October 3): the wisps and wraiths are hers; the wild's own creatures grow more upset the closer the party comes to her.

**Where these come from:** most are Chris's own creatures from his Aethermoor games and his D&D campaign, brought into this story: the Thornhound, Rime Wolf, Glowcap, Gorrow, Old Snag, Ember Beetle, Thunder-Roc, Blackwater Gar and Old Jaws (Hearth & Heirloom's bestiary, his Thareia notes), the White Hart of Fawnrest, and the hooks on his D&D map ("the forest animals are fleeing something" at Fawnrest, "the marsh creatures are getting bolder" at Willowmurk, "something large, heading this way" at Stormwatch). Here they belong to this game's world: Noctara's cold is what upsets them, not anything from the other games. None of them is canon until Chris places it.

## How to make them

1. Each creature has two prompts: a **model sheet** (how it looks from every side) and an **action sheet** (eight poses). Each prompt is ready to paste.
2. Generate each sheet two to four times and keep the one where the views agree best.
3. Save them as `<id>-model.png` and `<id>-actions.png` (the ids are in the table).
4. Take both sheets to a new modeling session with the creature's starter message from `../../envoi-final-draft/model-studio/README.md`. The model follows the sheets, not these words, so change anything here before generating.

**Wave 1 first:** bands 1 and 2 have no creatures of the wild at all yet, and the Thornhound's frost-maddened form fills band 4 too. Waves 2 and 3 give each band a great creature to find at one of the D&D map's named places, and more variety.

## Rules for all of them

- **Theirs, not hers.** They are animals and growing things of the wild. No undead, no shadow, no souls. Moths are souls going home, so none of them hunts or eats moths.
- **Upset by the cold.** Every creature gets a **frost-touched** look in its sheets: frost and rime on it, its breath steaming, its colours paler, its glow dimmer, its eyes wilder. The game uses more of it the closer the party comes to Noctara.
- **Beaten, not killed.** The game's tone rule: defeat is release, not death. Each one's last pose is how it leaves: slinking off, sitting down to sleep, flying away.
- **Colours** stay out of the other lanes: Noctara's deep purple, the wraiths' soul-green, Halcyon's cold blue, Io's magenta. Frost's pale blue-white is fine. Warm glowing accents read best at night.
- **Size** matters on the battle camera, which sits far off with a long lens: nothing smaller than about a meter tall, or it shrinks to a few pixels on the phone.

## Style lock

Every prompt below starts with the creature style lock that the Emberback's and the Gloamwing's sheets used.

## 2. The Glowcap (wave 1)

**In a fight:** with other creatures. Glow heals the others, Spore Puff makes a hero drowsy (her gauge fills slower), and they go for whoever glows most: Sol with her Heat high, or Io as she casts. Fire hurts them most.

## 9. The Blackwater Gar, and Old Jaws (wave 3)

Chris's Aethermoor: the Blackwater Gar, "all jaw and armour… then it leaps", and Old Jaws with his Death Roll; on his map, at Rotbridge, "something enormous… in the Blackwater". Here: long armored fish of the Gloomfen's black water, hunting anything that comes to the water's edge now the fen is dark. Old Jaws lives under Rotbridge. Beaten, a gar slides back under the black water and is gone.

**In a fight:** one or two, from a pool in the battlefield. Leap (out of the water onto a hero), Snap, and Dive (it goes under and can't be reached until it surfaces). Old Jaws fights alone and adds a Death Roll.
