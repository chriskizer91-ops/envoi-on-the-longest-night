# Envoi at Moonset: the story (a proposal)

November 16, 2026. Written by Claude for Chris. He asked for one story that settles every open lore question and uses what is already built.

**This is a proposal. Chris's word beats all of it.** Nothing in the game or on the board has changed. Lines marked **New** are things I made up to join the pieces. Everything else is already Chris's, from the lore documents, the repositories and the board.

## The story in seven lines

1. Auros, the moon, takes 500 years to circle Thareia. Every land gets about 250 years of moonlight, then about 250 without. (Chris's.)
2. **New:** the Moon Faire follows the moon, so it is the last thing a land sees before its moon sets. Its coming means goodbye. This game is Aethermoor's last moonlit season.
3. **New:** eight years ago Halcyon, a paladin and Io's older sister, went north to stop the dark from coming. At Misthollow's Moonwell she lit a fire that would never go out.
4. The fire pulls in every light: lamp flames, the warmth of the Ember Line, and the souls of the dead. It holds them and never lets them go home.
5. Io, who can't let go of anyone, and Kore, who has never held on to anything, cross Aethermoor together to reach it.
6. Kore's Curtain Call gives the fire its ending. Io's letters, sent at last, call each held soul home by name.
7. The moon sets. For the first time in 250 years the sky fills with stars. Io leaves home with the Faire.

Working title: **Envoi at Moonset**. An envoi is a send-off, and this story has three: for the dead, for the moon, and for Io leaving home.

## The one idea

**The dark is not the enemy. A light that won't let go is.**

Chris asked that darkness mean something different in this game. Here the dark is a season, like winter. It comes to every land in turn, and people live through it. The danger is someone who loves too much to let the night come.

That is Halcyon's fire. It is also Io's own flaw, grown to the size of a mountain, so the last boss is the heroine's weakness given a shape. And Kore, whose power is the night sky, is the dark as a mercy.

## The world's rules, one answer each

- **A soul is a light.** When someone dies, their light rises as a pale moth and flies to Auros. Lunara, the moth goddess, gathers them. (From the first Envoi.)
- **The road below. New:** when a land's moon has set, Auros is under the horizon. The dead of a dark land have to go the long way, under the world, and souls get lost on that road. "Below" and "the moon" are the same place by two roads. Kore is the one who walks the lost along it.
- **Moonwells** hold saved moonlight. Each is a door Lunara can come through, so the dead of a dark land don't have to walk the road below. **New:** this is what keepers are for. When the moon sets, a keeper stays and tends her well through the dark years.
- **A wisp** is a soul's light that has lost its way. It wants light because it is looking for the moon. **A Shadow Wraith** is a wisp that has forgotten it was ever a light. **The great wraith** is one that has swallowed a whole town's lights. A beaten one is released, and a moth rises.
- **One answer for both games.** Moon Faire's "flames that went cold" and this game's "souls going hollow" are the same thing, because a soul is a light.
- **Why there are so many now. New:** the fire in the north is calling every light. Souls that answer it instead of the moon go cold on the way. Those are the wisps.
- **Sunstone and the Ember Line** are old sunlight in the ground. The fire drinks that warmth too. So the Warm Roads are cold, the Magpie can't fly far without more crystal, and the land gets colder the nearer you come to the fire. It is a fire that gives no heat. It only takes.
- **The Aurosi** live on Auros the way sailors live on a ship. They don't own it. **New:** on the moon it snows moths. They know moonsets well, because they watch one land after another go dark. Their shipyard is selling the last passages out.
- **Magic.** Each witch comes to hers in her own way, which is why they need each other. What they share is that none of them takes it: Io asks, Kore witnesses, Europa mends, Callisto waits. Moon phases stay out of it, as Chris ruled.

## The people

**Io, the Herbal Witch.** She keeps Wickhollow's Moonwell. **New:** at moonset a keeper is meant to do two things. She sends up the land's letters to the dead, the last post before the dark. Then she stays with her well for life. Io has kept every letter and can't send them. The oldest one in the bundle is to her sister. Her fire spell is the family gift, the same fire as Halcyon's, which is why she hates using it.

**Kore, the Jester.** The Faire's fool. How she went below: **she died on stage in the middle of a last show, and nobody in the house knew.** Below, she was offered a bargain: walk the lost home, and you may take your bow. So she walks back out every year, and every year she has to go back. (That is A and C together.)

She can see how every story ends. **New: the fire stops endings, so she can't see anything it holds.** On the first night she looks at Io, as she looks at everyone, and for the first time in her life she sees nothing. That is why she stays. For this whole game, the one who knows everything is guessing, and she finds she cares how it comes out.

**Halcyon. New: Io's older sister.** She was a paladin of the Ember Wardens (kept from the first game, with a new job: they keep the Warm Roads lit through the dark years). She kept the well before Io and taught her everything, which is why Io prays like a paladin. She went north so her little sister would never have to spend her life as one small light in a 250-year night. Io thinks she is dead. Each sister has been holding on to the other.

- Her face is lost in light because she has given herself to the vow. When the light finally goes out, the face under it is nearly Io's.
- Her model is Io's body, and her moves are Io's moves. In the story that is the point: **she fights with Io's own spells, because she taught them.** "She taught me that."
- The name already fits. Halcyon days are the calm days of midwinter, when the wind is held still. In the old story Alcyone would not be parted from her drowned husband. It is a grief that stops time.

**The Vigil** (my name for "the fire that would not end", which stays as its title). Halcyon lit it in Misthollow's Moonwell, with the well's own moonlight as kindling. That is why that well is dead. Her plan for moonset is to raise it into the sky as a moon that never sets. It is a black sphere in a corona because it is a moon made of held lights. It is no longer hers: it has grown its own keeper's hands, the two iron hands on the model.

- **The four fair lanterns** on its rim are the Moon Faire's house lights. It took them at the Faire's last show. That is Kore's own reason to go: a show can't end until the house lights come up.
- **The one candle** at its heart is the small flame Halcyon carried from home.

**Lunara** is Io's faith from before the story. **The Elderwood Guardian** comes once Kore holds the line. **Envoi, the Letter Wyrm**, is folded from the unsent letters and lit by Kore's bells.

**The Grey Hat**, the Faire's airship captain, holds the gangway until moonset and not a minute after. He is the clock, and he is the way out at the end. (Moon Faire's brief says the four meet him. If that is their first meeting, he stays out of this game.)

## The story, zone by zone

No place, fight or level moves. These are new reasons for what is built.

**Zone 1 (levels 1 to 5): Wickhollow, the Thornwood, Bogmire.**

- The Faire has played its last show. Its ship lifts. One fool stays behind.
- Io fights the first wraith alone in the square. Then bells. Kore ends a wisp, and a moth rises. Io has never seen a lost one go home.
- That night Io tells Kore about the letters. It is the first secret in the pendant, and the one Kore never spends.
- Bogmire is already dark. The great wraith sits in the fen with the town's lamps in its ribs, too heavy with light to carry them north.
- When it is released, the lamps fly home. But every flame in Bogmire leans north with no wind. Kore: "That's not the way home."

**Zone 2 (levels 5 to 10): the Warm Roads and Dawnroost.**

- The roads are cold. Io relights each dark node with a small flame she tends. She is learning that a kept fire is not a bad fire.
- Dawnroost is the Wardens' hall. **New:** forty went north with Halcyon. Marta still makes up forty bunks. "Io? Little Io? You have her walk."
- Wraiths come for the last living node. After that fight Io folds the letters into the wyrm, and Kore's bells light its heart.

**Zone 3 (levels 10 to 15): the northern wilds, the crossroads, the shipyard.**

- The Bramble Horrors get bigger the farther north you go. **New:** they are prayers that were planted and never tended. Halcyon planted them all the way north. They fear fire because fire took their keeper.
- The ambush. A knight of light comes down the north road. Kore's Fate won't seal her: "She has no ending." The knight casts Thornwake, then Bramble Cradle. She stops when Io answers with the same spell, and walks back north.
- At the shipyard, Ysmera says what the Aurosi know: every land tries this once. She names the knight. Io learns her sister is alive.

**Zone 4 (levels 15 to 20): the frozen pass, Misthollow, the dead Moonwell.**

- The cold is the fire's doing. The Bramble Colossus by the frozen road is the last and biggest prayer, set to bar the way. Its heart-seed is a guardian that was never called home.
- Misthollow is high in the peaks, so it keeps the moon longest. Under the fire it is never dark and never warm. No flame will stay lit there. Nobody has been able to grieve in eight years.
- On the Moonwell stair, Halcyon. Io hands her the first letter. It is the only one that can't be sent up, because she isn't dead. The light over her face goes out. (This can be the finale's first stage, or a scene with no fight.)
- The Vigil, whole. Nothing hurts it, because nothing in it can end. Kore calls it onto herself and takes everything it has. Curtain Call gives it an ending, and it breaks open.
- The Vigil, broken open. Envoi's last strike is the real sending. The letters burn for good, and each held soul follows its own name out. Eight years of moths go up at once.
- One small flame is left in the bowl. Io doesn't put it out. It becomes the lamp in Misthollow's well. The four lanterns light again.

**The ending.** Auros goes down. The stars come out, more than anyone alive in Aethermoor has ever seen. Halcyon carries a taper home to keep Wickhollow's well. Io goes up the gangway with Kore and one of the four lanterns. The sister who left comes home. The sister who stayed leaves.

## What each town believes (for the townsfolk's words)

- **Wickhollow** acts as if moonset isn't coming. Everyone is busy.
- **Bogmire** went dark early and found out you can live in the dark. Wenna knows her jars by touch.
- **Dawnroost** keeps forty beds made for people who aren't coming back.
- **The shipyard** is leaving, and selling the way out.
- **Misthollow** got what Halcyon wanted for everyone: no dark, and no loss. It is the saddest town in the game.

Five towns, five answers to an ending. That gives every person something to say.

## The bigger story: the four

**New, and the part where I most want Chris's own word.** He has given the four powers as four sizes: the moment, nature, chaos and infinity. My reading is that each one answers something that refuses to end.

- **Callisto, the moment:** a moment that won't pass.
- **Io, nature:** a growth nobody tends.
- **Europa, chaos:** a pattern that can't change.
- **Kore, infinity:** a life that won't end.

So the same trouble can come back in every land the moon leaves, at a different size each time, and it takes a different witch to answer it. This game is Io's and Kore's. Moon Faire's demo story already gives each of the four an act, and it stands as it is: a second fire, in another land, years later. Kore: "We've met its sister."

How the other two might join. A sketch only:

- **Europa** is already with the Faire. She sewed its stage curtain, the one the fire took and burned, and her first words to Io and Kore are about what they did to it. Nobody at the Faire has met her twice, because she wears borrowed faces. She has been stitching the Faire's players into a new curtain. Kore is in it. There were two blank places, and one now has a red hat.
- **Callisto** is a princess of the next land the Faire reaches. In the old story the Great Bear is the one who saw. She sees what the others are still guessing at.
- **The four lanterns** end up one to each witch.
