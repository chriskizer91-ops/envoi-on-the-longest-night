# The Coven's Names: Io, Kore, Europa and Callisto

Nov 16, 2026 · Chris. Brought into this project from Chris's upload, with one change: the pairing below is now set, because Chris confirmed it on November 16. How the names feed Io's and Kore's lore: `claude/lore/io-and-kore.md`.

## How to read this

Io, Kore, Europa and Callisto are the four witches' own names. Thareia has no moons by those names. The names point back to real-world myth, as Io's already did in Envoi. What ties the four to the sky is Auros, the moon they follow round Thareia with the Moon Faire.

Chris confirmed this pairing on November 16. The table lists the witches in the lore's order of magic, strongest first:

| Name | Witch | Her power |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kore | the Jester | the duality of life and death, and infinity |
| Europa | the Fox | chaos, and the fabric of reality |
| Io | the Herbal Witch | healing |
| Callisto | the Ranger | the moment |

Under each name, the real-world meanings run from most relevant to the story to least. "The story" means what Chris has set so far: four witches who follow a moon, a fair that comes round once in 500 years, and their four powers.

## Kore, the Jester

**In the Moon Faire:** the strongest magic of the four and the most HP. She is the tank: she taunts the worst foes onto herself, and every blow aimed at her charges the spell that ends the fight. Her power is the duality of life and death, and infinity.

**What her name carries, most relevant first:**

1. **The queen of the dead who comes back.** Kore ("the Maiden") is Persephone. Carried below the earth, she ate pomegranate seed there, so she spends part of every year with the dead and the rest among the living. She holds both sides of life and death, every year, forever.
2. **The moon as the border of life and death.** In Plutarch's *On the Face in the Moon*, Persephone, also called Cora, is mistress of the moon. The moon is "sovereign over life and death": souls go there after dying and die a second time. A Kore who follows a moon fits this almost exactly.
3. **A performer who moved her.** Orpheus sang before Hades and Persephone, and his song moved them to let Eurydice go. The queen of the dead was moved by a performer, which suits a Jester.
4. **The Mysteries.** Her story founds the Eleusinian Mysteries, secret rites that promised initiates a blessed afterlife. She knows something about death that she only shares with those inside.
5. **The girl in the eye.** In Greek, *kore* also means the pupil of the eye, from the tiny figure you see reflected there.
6. **The moon Kore.** A tiny moon of Jupiter, named in 2007, among the farthest out, and it orbits backwards. The strongest witch is named for a speck at the edge going the other way.

## Europa, the Fox

**In the Moon Faire:** the chaos witch, a playful fabric artist who sews reality with scissors that become daggers, needle, thread and a bell. Her foxfire is cold blue. She turns foes on themselves, confuses them, and gets the party out of fights.

**What her name carries, most relevant first:**

1. **The first picture in the most famous tapestry in myth.** When the weaver Arachne challenged the goddess Athena, the first scene she wove was Europa on the bull. Ovid says you would have taken the bull and the waves for real. Her name is the first thing a mortal artist made so real that it angered the gods.
2. **Carried where no one could follow.** A white bull carried her from Tyre across the sea to Crete, where she became its first queen. Her brothers searched and never found her. The Fox is the one who opens a way out that foes cannot follow.
3. **Wide eyes.** Her name is usually read as Greek *eurys*, "wide", and *ops*, "eye" or "face": "wide-gazing" or "broad-faced".
4. **Gifts that never miss.** Zeus gave her Talos, a bronze guardian; Laelaps, a hound that always caught its prey; and a javelin that never missed. Tools that are more than tools, like scissors that become daggers.
5. **A continent.** In the best-known telling, Europe takes its name from her.
6. **The moon Europa.** A shell of ice crossed by cracks thousands of kilometres long, over what is likely an ocean. It looks like a frozen surface covered in seams.

Sources: [Arachne's tapestry, Ovid *Metamorphoses* 6 (Theoi)](https://ftp.theoi.com/Heroine/Arakhne.html) · [Europa (consort of Zeus)](https://Www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rape_of_Europa) · [Europa and Europe's name (Greek City Times)](https://greekcitytimes.com/2022/03/05/europa-greek-europe/) · [Galilean landscapes, NASA JPL](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/images/pia00743-landscape-comparisons-galilean-satellites/) · [Europa's ocean (Planetary Society)](https://planetary.org/articles/what-are-jupiters-galilean-moons)

## Io, the Herbal Witch

**In the Moon Faire:** the healer in the red hat. Her heals are strong, her attacks are light, and her summons call deities with a paladin's energy. She came from Chris's earlier games. In Envoi she was named after the io moth and the moon Io.

**What her name carries, most relevant first:**

1. **Io means "moon".** Greek lexicons record Io as the name of the moon at Argos, and ancient writers read her story as the moon's. In Aeschylus she calls herself "the horned virgin", horned like a crescent. A witch who follows a moon is named Moon.
2. **The wanderer.** Hera sent a gadfly that drove her across the world. The Bosporus ("ox-ford") and the Ionian Sea were said to be named after her crossings, until she reached Egypt. She is a traveller, like the fair she travels with.
3. **Restored, then worshipped.** In Egypt, Zeus restored her to her own form. Later tellings say she ruled so kindly that she was worshipped as the goddess Isis. That's a healer who becomes divine herself.
4. **The violet.** Gaia made the violet (Greek *ion*) so the heifer would have something to eat, and the flower was said to take her name. That's folk etymology, but it gives the herbal witch a flower of her own.
5. **Watched by a hundred eyes.** Hera set Argus, a giant with a hundred eyes, to guard her. The io moth she was named after in Envoi has eye-spots on its wings.
6. **Mother of a line.** Prometheus, chained in the Caucasus, foretold that her descendant would free him. Her line runs to Perseus, Cadmus, Heracles, Minos, and Europa.
7. **The moon Io.** The most volcanically active world in the solar system, splashed red and yellow with sulphur.

## Callisto, the Ranger

**In the Moon Faire:** a princess with a bow, a sword and knives. Her magic is the weakest of the four. Her plain attacks are her strength, and her magic makes them stronger. Her power is the moment.

**What her name carries, most relevant first:**

1. **A huntress and a king's daughter.** In Hesiod she is the daughter of Lycaon, king of Arcadia, sworn to hunt in the band of Artemis, the archer goddess. Like the Ranger, she's a princess with a bow.
2. **A story that turns on one moment.** Turned into a bear, she was nearly shot by her own son while he hunted. Zeus stopped the shot and lifted them both into the sky. Her whole myth hangs on one instant held still.
3. **The bear that never sets.** She became the Great Bear. Hera had the sea gods bar her from the ocean, so the Bear circles the North Star and never sinks below the horizon. She is always there, every night.
4. **She followed the moon.** Artemis came to be identified with Selene, the moon. Callisto's life was spent following the moon goddess through the wilds.
5. **The witness.** When Demeter asked the stars who had taken her daughter, the Great Bear answered and sent her on to ask further. The Bear watches all night, and she tells what she sees.
6. **"Most beautiful".** Her name comes from Greek *kalliste*, "the most beautiful".
7. **The moon Callisto.** The most heavily cratered body in the solar system. Its surface has barely changed in about 4 billion years, so every blow that ever landed on it is still there.

## The four names together

The strongest link is the one the story already has: each name follows the moon in its own way.

1. **Four ways of following a moon.** Io *is* the moon, by the old Argive word. Kore *rules* it, as queen of the souls who go there in Plutarch. Callisto *served* it, hunting in the band of the goddess who became the moon. Europa's brother, searching for her, was told to follow a cow with a white full moon on each flank, and founded Thebes where it lay down. So all four names follow a moon, and so does the coven.
2. **Two pairs.** Io and Europa are family: Europa is Io's great-great-granddaughter, so the coven holds an ancestor and her descendant. Callisto and Kore are joined by a search: when Demeter asked the stars about her stolen daughter, the Great Bear answered.
3. **One book, one tapestry.** Ovid's *Metamorphoses* tells all four stories: Io in book 1, Callisto and Europa in book 2, Proserpina (Kore) in book 5. Arachne's tapestry in book 6 weaves two of them, Europa and Proserpina. The Fox's own tapestry already has the Jester in it.
4. **Changed shapes, crossed borders.** Io became a heifer and Callisto a bear. Europa was carried over the sea, and Kore below the earth. Every one of them changes form or crosses between worlds, which suits a coven of witches.
5. **Flowers.** Europa and Kore were both gathering flowers in a meadow when they were taken. In Ovid, Kore was picking violets, the flower named for Io.
6. **Eyes.** A hundred-eyed giant guarded Io. Europa's name means "wide-eyed", and *kore* is the pupil of the eye. The Great Bear never sets, so she never stops watching.
7. **Isis.** Io was later worshipped as Isis, and a Hermetic text called *Kore Kosmou*, "Maiden of the World", has Isis teaching how the world was made. That makes Io's and Kore's names meet a second time.

## Where else the names turn up

1. **Jupiter's moons.** In 1614 Simon Marius, on Johannes Kepler's suggestion, named Jupiter's four big moons after Zeus's loves: Io, Europa, Ganymede and Callisto. Galileo refused those names, but they're the ones that stuck. The coven swaps Ganymede, a boy in the myth, for Kore, which keeps the four all women. Kore is a Jupiter moon too, a tiny one.
2. **Europa in the news.** NASA's Europa Clipper launched on 14 October 2024 and reaches Jupiter in April 2030 to study the ocean under Europa's ice. Many players will know Europa and Io as moons before they know the myths.
3. **A near echo in anime.** In *Sailor Moon*, the Amazoness Quartet are four girls named after the first four asteroids ever found. They perform in the Dead Moon Circus, which arrives on a flying ship. The names are different, but the shape is close: four girls with sky names, travelling by air with a show named for the moon. It's worth knowing about while the Moon Faire's look and pitch are being set.

## Open points

- **The pairing is set.** Chris confirmed Kore as the Jester, Europa as the Fox and Callisto as the Ranger on November 16.
- **The myths are real-world references.** Thareia doesn't have to know any of them. Which meanings become in-world lore, and how, is Chris's call.
- **The shared dark thread.** In every one of these myths, a god takes her: Zeus takes Io, Europa and Callisto, and Hades takes Kore. Arachne's tapestry was a true record of exactly that. The game could make it a theme, four women who now tell their own stories, or keep it offstage. Either works, but it's better to choose on purpose than to have players point it out.
