# Io and Kore: the lore (draft)

Envoi, the second game. Draft of November 16, 2026, written with Chris. Nothing here is final, and Chris's word beats all of it. Lines marked **Open** are still his to decide.

The page with pictures of their 3D models: https://claude.ai/artifact/T5FY92Qit3GrDFPqhyVK5o (also sent to Chris as one HTML file, `io-and-kore-lore.html`). What all four names mean in myth: `claude/lore/coven-names.md`.

## This game

This is Envoi, but not *On the Longest Night*. It's a second game in an alternate universe, and it doesn't have to agree with anything in the first game. It borrows that game's places, foes, arenas and models, and uses what we've learned since. Its story, its lore and the townsfolk's words are all written new.

## The coven quartet

The coven is four witches of the Moon Faire who work together. They're listed here with the strongest magic first. Chris settled which name goes with which witch on November 16.

Their names come from real-world myth, as Io's already did in the first Envoi. Thareia has no moons by those names. What ties the four to the sky is Auros, the moon they follow round Thareia with the Moon Faire.

The bigger story is how they come to know each other. In this game only the first two have met. In *Moon Faire*, all four travel together and share a past.

- **Kore, the Jester** (in this game). Her power is the duality of life and death, and infinity. She has the strongest magic of the four and the most HP: she is the tank. Her name is Persephone's. Persephone was queen of the dead, and she comes back every spring, forever.
- **Europa, the Fox** (not in this game). Her power is chaos, and the fabric of reality. She is a playful fabric artist who sews reality with scissors, needle, thread and a bell, and her foxfire is cold blue. In myth, Europa on the bull was the first scene in Arachne's tapestry, woven so real it angered the gods.
- **Io, the Herbal Witch** (in this game). Her power is healing. Her heals are strong, her attacks are light, and her summons have a paladin's energy. In the old Greek of Argos, Io meant "moon". In the first Envoi she was also named after the io moth.
- **Callisto, the Ranger** (not in this game). Her power is the moment. She is a princess with a bow, a sword and knives. Her magic is the weakest of the four, and it makes her plain attacks stronger. In myth, she was a huntress whose whole story turns on one moment held still.

A real-world nod: Io, Europa and Callisto are three of Jupiter's four big moons, and Kore is a tiny one. The coven swaps in Kore for Ganymede, who is a boy in the myth, so all four are women. Nobody on Thareia knows any of this.

## Where their magic comes from

- **Io's magic is asked for, never owned.** Her power lives in what she tends and in the promises she keeps. Plants answer her because she cares for them. The old powers answer her because she has made vows to them, and she keeps those vows like a paladin. That is why her biggest spells are prayers, and why a prayer takes a moment to arrive.
- **Kore's magic is endings.** She can see how every story ends. She can't change that ending, but she writes down the choices people make on the way to it. Whoever chooses to hurt her writes a little more of their own ending. When a foe's Fate catches up with its Life, its story is finished. She ends it, and the soul goes home as a pale moth instead of going hollow.
- **The foes.** A wisp is a soul that is starting to go hollow, and it craves light. A Shadow Wraith is what a wisp becomes when nobody helps it. A beaten foe isn't killed. It is released, and a pale moth rises.

**Open:** is there one source of magic for the whole coven, or does each witch come to hers in her own way?

## Io, the Herbal Witch: she holds on

- **Power:** healing. Strong heals, light attacks, and summons with a paladin's energy.
- **Look:** a wine-red horned hat, glasses, a spellbook, and a red robe grown over with green vines.
- **Hidden weapon:** her crescent pendant unfolds into a golden harvest sickle.
- **In the fight:** 1,400 HP and 120 MP at level 1. Everything grows about 20% a level.
- **Trance:** she glows silver-green.

**Who she is.** Io is a wandering healer. She knows what it is to be far from home, which is part of why she stays with a travelling fair. She has saved a great many people, but not all of them. She can't let go of the ones she lost, so she writes them letters she has nowhere to send. That's her weight. Her vow is simple: she takes nothing that isn't freely given. She would rather hold a foe than hurt it.

**What her name carries.**
- **Io means "moon".** It was the moon's name in the old Greek of Argos. In Aeschylus she calls herself "the horned virgin", horned like a crescent, and Io wears a horned hat.
- **The wanderer.** In myth she was driven across the world, and the seas she crossed were said to take her name. She travels with a fair.
- **The violet.** The violet (Greek *ion*) was said to be named for her, so the herbal witch has a flower of her own. **Open:** should the violet be Io's flower in the game, in her spells or on her robe?
- **A healer made divine.** Restored to her own shape, she was later worshipped as the goddess Isis.
- **A hundred eyes.** The giant Argus watched her with a hundred eyes, and the io moth she was named for in the first Envoi has eye-spots on its wings.

**Her moves.** The numbers are at level 1. In *Moon Faire* the same lore holds, and some moves have short names there: Thorn, Mend, Bloom, Deity and Revive.

Her sickle:

- **Harvest Crescent** (free): the sickle hides in her pendant, because a healer shouldn't be seen carrying a blade. It's a harvesting tool, not a weapon. When she cuts with it, she's cutting away what has gone wrong so the rest can grow. *One wide cut: 265 to one foe.*
- **Crescent Blades** (24 MP): the sickle's arc, thrown as light, five times over. *Five cuts of 88 moon damage, 440 in all.*

What grows:

- **Thornwake** (24 MP): her one herb spell that harms, and even this one mostly holds. Roots burst up and pin the foe in place: you stake and bind what grows the wrong way. *340 to one foe, and the foe is Bound: its turn gauge drops and refills at half speed until its next turn.*
- **Nightbloom Briars** (18 MP): night-blooming briars that hold a foe the same way. *260 to one foe, and the foe is Bound.*
- **Bramble Cradle** (10 MP): her guard. Brambles curl round her like a hedge round a nest. Touching a healer costs you. *Until her next turn, blows on her are halved, and a third of each comes back on the striker as thorns.*
- **Flame Bolt** (12 MP): the one thing a gardener truly fears is fire. Io uses it the way a gardener burns blight, to stop a rot before it spreads. The fire was always in her. She just doesn't like using it. *330 fire to one foe.* **Open:** a hook for the story. The final boss is a fire that won't end, and the thing Io fears most is a fire she can't put out.

What heals:

- **Lunar Mend** (16 MP): medicine, quick and plain. *Heals one friend 380. In her trance it heals both of them.*
- **Silverleaf Salve** (22 MP): medicine that takes longer to prepare and does more. *Heals one friend 560.*
- **Waxing Light** (24 MP): she gardens people. *Heals both 270.*
- **Moonflower Garden** (32 MP): seeds take root round every friend and bloom. *Heals both 380.*
- **Rootbound Return** (24 MP): roots grow down, toward the place below where Kore walks, and pull a fainted friend back up. It's the one thing Kore lets anyone take back from her side. *A fainted friend gets up with 30% of her HP.*

Her gifts to Kore:

- **Moonsteel** (18 MP): Io tends her friends' gear the way she tends her tools, and she whets Kore's flail. *Kore's next blow strikes as moon. Wisps and wraiths take half again as much from moon.*
- **Harvest Moon** (28 MP): in farm lore, the harvest moon is when everything that was sown gets gathered in, so each foe reaps what it has sown. *For Kore's next three moves, every blow she lands writes Fate on its foe, half as much as a blow on her writes. This comes later in the story.*

Her prayers:

- **The Elderwood Guardian** (once a battle): a stag of living wood, an old forest power she has kept faith with. She plants her prayer like a seed, and a sapling becomes a fawn, then the stag, because prayers, like seeds, take a moment to come up. She calls him once a fight, because you don't ask a god twice in one day. He only comes once Kore has joined. Io can't plant a prayer while she's alone: someone has to hold the line while it grows, and Kore does. *The stag charges for 1,250 moon damage and bursts into vines that hold the foe.*
- **Lunara** (once a battle): the moth goddess. Every soul that's released becomes a pale moth, and Lunara gathers them. Io prayed to her for the dying long before she met Kore. *Her Embrace heals both witches 60% of their HP, brings a fainted friend back with 60%, and cuts blows on the party by 40%. On Io's next turn comes Silver Requiem: six beams of 150, then 1,150.*
- **Envoi, the Letter Wyrm** (once a battle): Io's unsent letters fold themselves into a great paper wyrm, and Kore's bells light the lantern of its heart. The heart only lights for Kore, because hers are the only bells heard on both sides, by the living and by the dead. In poetry, an envoi is the last stanza: the send-off. The wyrm is the letters finally being sent. It's the one sun-thing in a night story, because the letters are full of remembered daylight. *Kore must be standing. Its Folding Ward takes the foes' next attack whole. When Io's gauge next fills, it strikes: eight blows of 180, then 1,800. This comes later in the story.*

When she lets go:

- **Moonlight** (trance): for once, Io stops holding everyone together and lets go. Letting go is the lesson Kore teaches her. *Her trance fills as she's hurt and lasts four turns. While it lasts, her Moonlore spells cost half MP. Moonlight ends it with three strikes of moon: 420, 380 and 450.*

## Kore, the Jester: she lets go

- **Power:** the duality of life and death, and infinity. The strongest magic of the four.
- **Look:** a black two-horned jester hat, sheer veils and a cape, purples, and a turquoise pendant.
- **Hidden weapon:** the gold bells at her hip are the head of a chain flail.
- **In the fight:** 2,000 HP and 60 MP at level 1 today (more to come: see Balance). She has no Guard: catching blows is how she defends.
- **Trance:** she glows purple. Her trance is the Doom Dance.

**Who she is.** Kore has been below, and she walked back out laughing. Since then she has seen how every story ends: her own, her friends', and her foes'. That's why she's the merriest witch on the Faire. She knows exactly how short everything is.

She's a licensed fool, the only one who may say true things to a lord's face. Death is the one truth everybody shares, so she laughs at it out loud. She goes the other way from everyone else. Her bells ring on both sides of the curtain, so nobody, living or dead, can sneak up on her. Everyone hears her coming, and nobody takes a jingling fool seriously.

Her weight is the opposite of Io's. She lets go too easily. She has never had anything she couldn't bear to lose, until Io. Her lesson is to defend so her friends survive.

**Her infinity** is the circle she lives in. She goes below and comes back, every year, forever. For Kore, no ending is ever the last one.

**What her name carries.**
- **The queen of the dead who comes back.** Persephone ate pomegranate seed below the earth, so she spends part of every year with the dead and the rest among the living.
- **The moon as the border.** In Plutarch, she rules the moon, which is "sovereign over life and death". Souls go there after dying. A Kore who follows a moon fits this almost exactly. **Open:** when a soul is released as a pale moth, does it fly up to Auros?
- **Moved by a performer.** Orpheus sang before the king and queen of the dead, and his song moved them to let Eurydice go. That suits a Jester.
- **The girl in the eye.** In Greek, *kore* also means the pupil of the eye, from the tiny figure you see reflected there.

**Open:** how did Kore go below?
- **A:** she died once, mid-show, and walked back out with her bells. (Claude's pick.)
- **B:** she was born in the hour between dusk and dark, with one foot on each side.
- **C:** she made a bargain: she walks lost souls home, and in return she was let go.

A and C can go together.

**Her rules: Fate.**

- **Fate on whoever strikes her:** every blow on her is a choice, and she writes it down. The striker has chosen a little more of its own ending, and the gold stripe on a foe's bar is that ending catching up with it. When Fate reaches the foe's Life, the foe is sealed. *Each blow on her writes 450 Fate on its striker, double for a big attack the foe warned of. Big foes take a third of that. About five blows seal a Shadow Wraith, and two seal a wisp.*
- **Peril:** Kore marks anyone who is close to taking a friend from her. This is where her lesson shows. *When Io, or Kore herself, drops below half her HP, every foe gains 5% of its Life as Fate. This happens once a fight for each of them.*
- **When she falls:** the writing fades when the hand holding the pen goes down. *Every foe's Fate halves, and nobody catches blows.*

**Her moves.**

- **Gilded Rebuke** (free): the flail made of her bells. A rebuke is what a fool gives a king: a plain, honest blow with no trick in it, so it doesn't taunt. *290 to one foe.*
- **Attack Taunt** (free): she capers and mocks the foes so they turn on her and leave Io alone. *330 to one foe, and she catches the next 3 single-target blows aimed at Io.*
- **Heal Taunt** (10 MP): she dances among the stars and heals herself while the foes keep swinging. Laughter is her medicine. She lives because she won't take death seriously. *She heals herself 290 and catches the next 3 blows.*
- **Sealed Fate** (free): once a foe's Fate meets its Life, its story is finished. She closes the seal and ends it, even with life still in it, and the soul is released as a moth instead of going hollow. That's the jester's mercy. One bell tolls, and everyone hears it. *It ends a sealed foe at once, whatever Life it has left. Every other foe loses a fifth of its own full Life.*
- **Curtain Call** (free): when the last story is ended, the show is over. Two curtains of night close, and anyone who fainted gets up for the bow. In a theatre, nobody who "died" on stage stays down at the end. Curtain Call is Kore refusing to let her friends' deaths be real. Then the next show starts, the way she always comes back. That's her infinity. *It's Sealed Fate on the last foe standing. The fight ends, and a fainted Io gets up with a quarter of her HP. It glows purple, with mist and pixel motes.*
- **Doom Dance** (trance): her purple trance, the curtain getting ready to close. *For four turns, every blow she lands writes Fate on its foe too, half as much as a blow on her writes.*
- **Lighting Envoi's heart:** only her bells can light the Letter Wyrm's heart.

## Io and Kore

- **What each gives the other.** Io gives Kore something worth defending, and Kore gives Io permission to let go. Each one's lesson is the other.
- **How their magic fits.** Io can heal the living, but she can't help a soul that's going hollow, and she has nowhere to send her letters. Kore can send souls home, but she has never held on to anything. Kore stands in front and catches the blows, and Io keeps her standing. Io's prayers have time to grow because Kore holds the line.
- **Not sisters.** They are very close friends, and that can grow into something deeper later.
- **A seed for later.** Kore's turquoise pendant holds the secrets she has been told. She can spend them, but there's one she never will: the first thing Io ever told her.

**How they meet (proposal).** On the night this game opens, Io is fighting alone (the first fight is hers alone). The wisps are coming, souls going hollow. She can heal the living, but she can't do anything for the dead.

Then she hears bells. A jester walks out of the dark, and Io sees death coming in motley and is afraid of her. Kore ends the first wisp, and a pale moth rises instead of a wraith.

The first thing they disagree about, whether to hold on or let go, turns out to be the first thing they need from each other. Their prayers can follow the friendship:
- Lunara is Io's own faith, from before they met.
- The Elderwood Guardian comes once someone guards Io while she prays.
- Envoi comes once Io trusts Kore with her letters.

Europa and Callisto meet them later, in the bigger story.

## Balance: Kore gets more HP

**Chris's call, November 16:** Kore gets more HP, a lot more. She's the Jester: she goads the foes into hitting her, so the way to win is to let her get hit as often as possible. She needs a very high HP, and her Fate should be extremely powerful, outrageously, overwhelmingly so.

Her rules work in *Moon Faire*, where she's one of four. Here she's one of two, so she catches more blows with less help. These are today's numbers, before the change:

| | Moon Faire | This game now |
|---|---|---|
| Party | Four witches | Two witches |
| Kore's HP | 1,000 | 2,000 |
| Io's HP | 420 | 1,400 |
| Kore's share of the party's HP | 44% (1,000 of 2,280) | 59% (2,000 of 3,400) |
| Fate per blow on her | 500 (a wisp has 300 Life, so one blow seals it) | 450 (a wisp has 1,100 Life, a Shadow Wraith 2,600) |
| Blows she catches per taunt | 3 | 3 |

### A first test of the idea (November 16)

Claude ran this game's balance checks (`moon-faire-witches/tools/balance.mjs`): every fight 200 times, with a careless, a careful and an expert player. These were tries; nothing in the game has changed yet. There are two fights to watch. A careless player should beat the great wraith 20 to 60% of the time, and an expert should win the finale 60 to 85% of the time.

| Try | Kore's HP | Fate per blow | Checks passed | Great wraith, careless | Finale, expert |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Today | 2,000 | 450 | 42 of 51 | 44% | 13% |
| More HP | 4,000 | 450 | 25 of 51 | 94% | 31% |
| Both way up | 5,000 | 1,300 | 22 of 51 | 100% | 100% |
| Both way up, and foes' one-on-one blows twice as hard | 5,000 | 1,300 | 30 of 51 | 87% | 95% |

- **What it shows.** Today, Kore is too weak for the finale: an expert wins it only 13% of the time. More HP helps there.
- **The catch.** With both turned way up, nothing is a threat any more. A careless player beats the great wraith every time, and the Bramble Colossus falls 98% of the time when a careful player should beat it only 15 to 40% of the time. A Fate of 1,300 seals a wisp in one blow and a Shadow Wraith in two.
- **Making the foes hit harder** brings some danger back. But it also crushes Io in the first fight, before Kore has joined: a careless player wins only 18% of the time instead of 45 to 75%.
- **The checks themselves** came from the first game, made for Sol. If Kore is meant to be overwhelming, this game may want checks of its own.

How to repeat a try, for the `fight-math` job: `MFW_TUNE='{"HEROES.jester.hp":5000,"FATE.perBlow":1300}' node moon-faire-witches/tools/balance.mjs --n 200`. For the try with harder blows, also scale every foe move whose target isn't `all` by 2 before running.

1. How did Kore go below? A, B or C.
2. Who are Io's letters to? One person she couldn't save, or everyone she has lost?
3. Is this game the story of how Io and Kore meet?
4. Is there one source of magic for the whole coven, or does each witch have her own?
5. Should the violet be Io's flower?
6. Do the released moths fly up to Auros?
7. The shared dark thread: in every one of the four myths a god takes her. Zeus takes Io, and Hades takes Kore. Should the game make it a theme, four women who now tell their own stories, or keep it offstage? It's better to choose on purpose than have players point it out.
8. Kore's balance: if she gets huge HP and outrageous Fate, what keeps the big fights dangerous? Harder-hitting foes, foes that dodge her taunts and go for Io, or new checks for this game?
