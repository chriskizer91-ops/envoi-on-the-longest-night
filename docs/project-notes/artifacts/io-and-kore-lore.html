Envoi, the second game · lore draft

Io and Kore

Two witches of the Moon Faire, and the first two of the coven to find each other. Io holds on. Kore lets go.

Draft of November 16, 2026, written with Chris. Nothing here is final, and Chris's word beats all of it. Lines marked Open are still his to decide.

This game. This is Envoi, but not On the Longest Night. It's a second game in an alternate universe. It doesn't have to agree with anything in the first game. It borrows that game's places, foes, arenas and models, uses what we've learned since, and gets a story, lore and townsfolk's words of its own.

The coven quartet

Four witches of the Moon Faire who work together, listed with the strongest magic first. Chris settled which name goes with which witch on November 16.

Their names come from real-world myth, as Io's already did in the first Envoi. Thareia has no moons by those names. What ties the four to the sky is Auros, the moon they follow round Thareia with the Moon Faire.

The bigger story is how they come to know each other. In this game, only the first two have met. In Moon Faire, all four travel together and share a past.

In this game
Kore
the Jester · the duality of life and death, and infinity

The strongest magic of the four, and the most HP: she is the tank. Her name is Persephone's. Persephone was queen of the dead, and she comes back every spring, forever.

Not in this game
Europa
the Fox · chaos, and the fabric of reality

A playful fabric artist who sews reality with scissors, needle, thread and a bell. Her foxfire is cold blue. In myth, Europa on the bull was the first scene in Arachne's tapestry, woven so real it angered the gods.

In this game
Io
the Herbal Witch · healing

Her heals are strong, her attacks are light, and her summons have a paladin's energy. In the old Greek of Argos, Io meant "moon". In the first Envoi she was also named after the io moth.

Not in this game
Callisto
the Ranger · the moment

A princess with a bow, a sword and knives. Her magic is the weakest of the four, and it makes her plain attacks stronger. In myth, she was a huntress whose whole story turns on one moment held still.

A real-world nod: Io, Europa and Callisto are three of Jupiter's four big moons, and Kore is a tiny one. The coven swaps in Kore for Ganymede, who is a boy in the myth, so all four are women. Nobody on Thareia knows any of this.

Where their magic comes from

Io's magic is asked for, never owned. Her power lives in what she tends and in the promises she keeps. Plants answer her because she cares for them. The old powers answer her because she has made vows to them, and she keeps those vows like a paladin. That's why her biggest spells are prayers, and why a prayer takes a moment to arrive.

Kore's magic is endings. She can see how every story ends. She can't change that ending, but she writes down the choices people make on the way to it. Whoever chooses to hurt her writes a little more of their own ending. When a foe's Fate catches up with its Life, its story is finished. She ends it, and the soul goes home as a pale moth instead of going hollow.

The foes fit this. A wisp is a soul starting to go hollow, and it craves light. A Shadow Wraith is what a wisp becomes when nobody helps it. A beaten foe isn't killed. It's released, and a pale moth rises.

Who she is

Io is a wandering healer. She knows what it is to be far from home, which is part of why she stays with a travelling fair.

She has saved a great many people, but not all of them. She can't let go of the ones she lost, so she writes them letters she has nowhere to send. That's her weight. Her vow is simple: she takes nothing that isn't freely given. She would rather hold a foe than hurt it.

What her name carries

  • Io means "moon". It was the moon's name in the old Greek of Argos. In Aeschylus she calls herself "the horned virgin", horned like a crescent. Io wears a horned hat.
  • The wanderer. In myth she was driven across the world, and the seas she crossed were said to take her name. She travels with a fair.
  • The violet. The violet (Greek ion) was said to be named for her, so the herbal witch has a flower of her own.
  • A healer made divine. Restored to her own shape, she was later worshipped as the goddess Isis.
  • A hundred eyes. The giant Argus watched her with a hundred eyes, and the io moth she was named for in the first Envoi has eye-spots on its wings.

Her prayers
The Elderwood GuardianOnce a battle

A stag of living wood, an old forest power she has kept faith with. She plants her prayer like a seed, and a sapling becomes a fawn, then the stag. Prayers, like seeds, take a moment to come up. She calls him once a fight, because you don't ask a god twice in one day.

He only comes once Kore has joined. Io can't plant a prayer while she's alone. Someone has to hold the line while it grows, and Kore does.

In the fight: the stag charges for 1,250 moon damage and bursts into vines that hold the foe (Bound).

LunaraOnce a battle

The moth goddess. Every soul that's released becomes a pale moth, and Lunara gathers them. Io prayed to her for the dying long before she met Kore.

In the fight: her Embrace heals both 60% of their HP, brings a fainted friend back with 60%, and cuts blows on the party by 40%. On Io's next turn, Silver Requiem: six beams of 150, then 1,150.

Envoi, the Letter WyrmOnce a battle

Io's unsent letters fold themselves into a great paper wyrm, and Kore's bells light the lantern of its heart. The heart only lights for Kore, because hers are the only bells heard on both sides, by the living and by the dead. In poetry, an envoi is the last stanza: the send-off. The wyrm is the letters finally being sent. It's the one sun-thing in a night story, because the letters are full of remembered daylight.

In the fight: Kore must be standing. Its Folding Ward takes the foes' next attack whole. When Io's gauge next fills, it strikes: eight blows of 180, then 1,800. Later in the story.

When she lets go
MoonlightTrance

For once, Io stops holding everyone together and lets go. Letting go is the lesson Kore teaches her.

In the fight: her trance fills as she's hurt and lasts four turns. Her Moonlore spells cost half MP while it lasts. Moonlight ends it with three strikes of moon: 420, 380 and 450.

Who she is

Kore has been below, and she walked back out laughing. Since then she has seen how every story ends: her own, her friends', and her foes'. That's why she's the merriest witch on the Faire. She knows exactly how short everything is.

She's a licensed fool, the only one who may say true things to a lord's face. Death is the one truth everybody shares, so she laughs at it out loud. She goes the other way from everyone else. Her bells ring on both sides of the curtain, so nobody, living or dead, can sneak up on her. Everyone hears her coming, and nobody takes a jingling fool seriously.

Her weight is the opposite of Io's. She lets go too easily. She has never had anything she couldn't bear to lose, until Io. Her lesson is to defend so her friends survive.

Her infinity is the circle she lives in. She goes below and comes back, every year, forever. For Kore, no ending is ever the last one.

What her name carries

  • The queen of the dead who comes back. Persephone ate pomegranate seed below the earth, so she spends part of every year with the dead and the rest among the living.
  • The moon as the border. In Plutarch, she rules the moon, which is "sovereign over life and death". Souls go there after dying. A Kore who follows a moon fits this almost exactly.
  • Moved by a performer. Orpheus sang before the king and queen of the dead, and his song moved them to let Eurydice go. That suits a Jester.
  • The girl in the eye. In Greek, kore also means the pupil of the eye, from the tiny figure you see reflected there.

Her rules: Fate

Fate on whoever strikes her

Every blow on her is a choice, and she writes it down. The striker has chosen a little more of its own ending. The gold stripe on a foe's bar is that ending catching up with it. When Fate reaches its Life, the foe is sealed.

In the fight: each blow on her writes 450 Fate on its striker, double for a big attack the foe warned of. Big foes take a third of that. About five blows seal a Shadow Wraith, and two a wisp.

Peril

Kore marks anyone who is close to taking a friend from her. This is where her lesson shows.

In the fight: when Io, or Kore herself, drops below half her HP, every foe gains 5% of its Life as Fate. Once a fight for each of them.

When she falls

The writing fades when the hand holding the pen goes down.

In the fight: every foe's Fate halves, and nobody catches blows.

Why she fights the way she does

Gilded RebukeFree

The flail made of her bells. A rebuke is what a fool gives a king: a plain, honest blow with no trick in it, so it doesn't taunt.

In the fight: 290 to one foe.

Attack TauntFree

She capers and mocks the foes so they turn on her and leave Io alone.

Heal Taunt10 MP

She dances among the stars and heals herself while the foes keep swinging. Laughter is her medicine. She lives because she won't take death seriously.

In the fight: heals herself 290, and she catches the next 3.

Sealed FateFree

Once a foe's Fate meets its Life, its story is finished. She closes the seal and ends it, even with life still in it. The soul is released as a moth instead of going hollow. That's the jester's mercy. One bell tolls, and everyone hears it.

In the fight: ends a sealed foe at once, whatever Life it has left. Every other foe loses a fifth of its own full Life.

Curtain CallFree

When the last story is ended, the show is over. Two curtains of night close, and anyone who fainted gets up for the bow. In a theatre, nobody who "died" on stage stays down at the end. Curtain Call is Kore refusing to let her friends' deaths be real. Then the next show starts, the way she always comes back. That's her infinity.

In the fight: Sealed Fate on the last foe standing. The fight ends, and a fainted Io gets up with a quarter of her HP. It glows purple, with mist and pixel motes.

Doom DanceTrance

Her purple trance: the curtain getting ready to close.

In the fight: for four turns, every blow she lands writes Fate on its foe too, half as much as a blow on her writes.

Lighting Envoi's heart

Only her bells can light the Letter Wyrm's heart. See Io's prayers.

Io and Kore

  • What each gives the other. Io gives Kore something worth defending. Kore gives Io permission to let go. Each one's lesson is the other.
  • How their magic fits. Io can heal the living, but she can't help a soul that's going hollow, and she has nowhere to send her letters. Kore can send souls home, but she has never held on to anything. Kore stands in front and catches the blows, Io keeps her standing, and Io's prayers have time to grow because Kore holds the line.
  • Not sisters. Very close friends, and that can grow into something deeper later.
  • A seed for later. Kore's turquoise pendant holds the secrets she has been told. She can spend them, but there's one she never will: the first thing Io ever told her.

How they meet

On the night this game opens, Io is fighting alone (the first fight is hers alone). The wisps are coming: souls going hollow. She can heal the living, but she can't do anything for the dead.

Then she hears bells. A jester walks out of the dark, and Io sees death coming in motley and is afraid of her. Kore ends the first wisp, and a pale moth rises instead of a wraith.

The first thing they disagree about, whether to hold on or let go, turns out to be the first thing they need from each other. Their prayers can follow the friendship: Lunara is Io's own faith, from before they met. The Elderwood Guardian comes once someone guards Io while she prays. Envoi comes once Io trusts Kore with her letters.

Europa and Callisto meet them later, in the bigger story.

Balance: Kore gets more HP

Chris's call, November 16. Kore gets more HP, a lot more. She's the Jester: she goads the foes into hitting her, so the way to win is to let her get hit as often as possible. She needs a very high HP, and her Fate should be extremely powerful, outrageously, overwhelmingly so.

Her rules work in Moon Faire, where she's one of four. Here she's one of two, so she catches more blows with less help. Today's numbers, before the change:

A first test of the idea

Claude ran this game's balance checks: every fight, 200 times each, with a careless, a careful and an expert player. These were tries, and nothing in the game has changed yet.

Two fights to watch: a careless player should beat the great wraith 20 to 60% of the time, and an expert should win the finale 60 to 85% of the time.

  • What it shows. Today, Kore is too weak for the finale: an expert wins it only 13% of the time. More HP helps there.
  • The catch. With both turned way up, nothing is a threat any more. A careless player beats the great wraith every time, and the Bramble Colossus, which a careful player should beat only 15 to 40% of the time, falls 98% of the time. A Fate of 1,300 seals a wisp in one blow and a Shadow Wraith in two.
  • Making the foes hit harder brings some danger back, but it also crushes Io in the first fight, before Kore has joined: a careless player wins it 18% of the time instead of 45 to 75%.
  • The checks themselves came from the first game, made for Sol. If Kore is meant to be overwhelming, this game may want checks of its own.
